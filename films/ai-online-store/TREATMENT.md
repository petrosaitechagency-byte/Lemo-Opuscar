# One Pair of Hands — treatment

**Brief.** 90.0 s, vertical 1080×1920, English. Style: Paper Pop-up Book (`styles/paper-popup/STYLE.md`). Topic: how AI helps a small business, told through one online store. Offline AI narrator (Kokoro), original score, English subtitles burned in plus `subtitles_en.srt`. No statistics and no claims that need a source; no real products, brands or AI tools named. Closing card in the client's own brand (Petros AI-Tech Agency: supplied logo files only, Deep Indigo #0B0F2A, Soft White #FAF6FF, Royal Purple #6A00FF, Neon Blue #00E5FF, Sora + DM Sans). No storyboard stop.

Frame rate: the style asks for smooth macro camera moves on ones (60 fps). Measured on this machine (software WebGL, 4 cores) a frame costs about 3 s even after optimisation, so 60 fps would take more than 4 hours to render: the film is rendered at **30 fps**, the standard rate for vertical social video.

## 1. Three candidate structures

1. **Twenty-four hours.** The book is one day in the shop, a page per stretch of hours, a paper clock on every backdrop; the AI is the night shift. Clear, but a clock structure makes the film about time, not about the person, and it needs six or more spreads to feel like a day: too many sets for 90 s on a phone screen.
2. **Two books facing each other.** Left book: the old way, in graphite on cream; right book: the same shop with AI, in colour; parcels and messages traded across the gap. A strong before/after, but two books side by side are tiny in a vertical frame, and the comparison would carry the film instead of a character.
3. **One pair of hands (chosen).** One night spread for the problem, one day spread for the help. Mira, who makes mugs and sells them online, is buried under small jobs at 2 a.m. She tugs a paper tab and the page turns by itself. On the day spread, four pull-tabs along the page edge slide out on their own, one per job, and each starts a little paper mechanism that keeps running. The page fills with motion until the shop runs itself and Mira is back at her potter's wheel. At 2 a.m. again, a message comes in, the answer types itself, and Mira is asleep.

**Why 3.** A pull-tab is the pop-up book's own picture of automation: a mechanism that, once set, keeps working without a hand. It shows AI as quiet help, not as a robot. It also gives four clear beats of the same shape, each one readable at phone size, and lets the score build itself: each tab adds an instrument. The 2 a.m. bookend gives the ending its echo. The opening image (a phone pinging in the dark), the ending (the book shut on a desk next to a real handmade mug) and the shape of the score (a groove that builds itself, then a lullaby) all come from the topic.

## 2. Logline and arc

**Logline.** At 2 a.m. a one-woman online mug shop is drowning in small jobs; one by one, the pop-up book's tabs start pulling themselves, until the shop runs on its own and its maker is back at her wheel, and, at 2 a.m., asleep.

- **Setup** (0–24 s): night spread. Pings, speech bubbles, a tower of chore cards next to Mira. "One pair of hands. A hundred little jobs." Silence.
- **Turn** (24–30 s): Mira tugs the tab marked AI. The page turns by itself, and day springs up.
- **Development** (30–60 s): four tabs (Messages, Listings, Stock, Orders) slide out on their own; four mechanisms start and keep running.
- **Peak** (60–70 s): everything runs together in time; Mira at her wheel. "AI doesn't make the mugs. It gives Mira back the time to make them." Rack focus from paper to the real mug on the desk.
- **Ending** (70–85 s): sun on a stick goes down, moon comes up, 2 a.m. again: a question comes in and is answered while she sleeps with the cat. The book closes over the lens.
- **Close** (85–90 s): hard cut on the closing thump to the Petros AI-Tech card.

## 3. Benchmark

- ***The Little Prince* (2015), its paper stop-motion sequences.** Learn: warm practical light raking across paper fibre; how fragile a flat paper figure feels under a real lens; a camera that breathes slowly and lets a paper face hold. Not taken: the prince, the fox, any design, shot or music.
- **IKEA "BookBook" (2014).** Learn: a humble paper object presented with the clarity and confidence of a tech launch: one feature per beat, clean product framing, a pause after each point. Not taken: the parody voice, the catalogue, the brand, the music.

## 4. Characters, sets, props

- **Mira**: paper actor about 4.5 cm tall. Warm brown skin, dark hair in a bun with a pencil through it, sky-blue shirt, mustard apron with clay smudges, navy trousers. Faces: tired, determined, delighted, asleep. Poses: head in hand at the table; reaching up and tugging a tab; holding up a phone to photograph a mug; seated at the wheel, hands on clay; asleep in an armchair.
- **The cat**: a ginger paper cat, curled asleep; on Mira's lap at the end. Its only gag: it naps through everything, including the 2 a.m. ping.
- **Spread 1, "Night kitchen"** (dominant hue: indigo and lilac night, warm lamplight yellow inside): window with paper moon, wall clock at 2:14, kitchen table with lamp, phone and a laptop, and a tower of chore cards (envelope, tag, mug, box).
- **Spread 2, "The workshop"** (dominant hue: clay and terracotta with cream, sage green and speckled blue mugs; recurring accents red and gold): backdrop = workshop wall with a big window onto a little paper town of customers' houses and a sun on a stick; shelves of mugs; ground = work table with phone, potter's wheel, packing table with a conveyor strip and a label printer. Four pull-tabs along the front page edge.
- **The room**: walnut desk, warm lamp light from the left, a soft room behind (HDRI), and one real object: a hand-thrown stoneware mug, speckled glaze, the same "Speckled Sky" mug as in the story, standing on the desk just past the page edge. The book: indigo cloth cover with a gold foil mug emblem (no text).

## 9. Subtitles and titles

- **Subtitles** (the style's narration subtitles, adapted to a phone screen): Fredoka SemiBold about 50 px, cream #FFF6E6 with a thick dark-brown outline #3A2418 and a soft shadow, centred, at most two lines. They sit at about 72 % of the frame height, not at the very bottom, so that social apps' own buttons and captions never cover them. Each held at least max(1.8 s, speech + 0.6 s). English only, so the single line takes the upper line's place and size.
- **In the paper world**: speech bubbles are white cards with an ink outline, typed on with blips (Fredoka). Tab labels and the split-flap slats in Lilita One. The listing tag in IM Fell English ("Speckled Sky Mug"). The clock face shows 2:14. No text on the book cover.
- **Brand card**, flat and not lit by the room, as the brand guidelines ask (no glow, shadow or gradient on the logo): Deep Indigo field, the supplied light-on-dark lockup (Soft White wordmark, Neon Blue star) in the upper middle, the tagline "Innovate smarter. ## 11. As built (changes made while producing)

- **Tab close-ups last 2.1 s each** (on the downbeats of bars 13, 16, 19 and 22) so every tab word can be read; the narration lines L07, L08 and L09 start a little later (38.9, 46.2, 53.6 s) and the mechanism beats follow them: photo flash 39.85, listing written 41.6–43.0, stamp 43.95, slats 47.6, note 49.8, labels from 55.3, parcels from 56.2 s.
- **Day to night is a sky wheel** (a volvelle) behind the workshop window instead of a sun on a stick: it turns half a revolution so the sun sets behind the paper town and the moon comes up, while the wall clock spins to 2:14. It fits inside the book; a sliding strip would have shown past the book's edges.
- **The card** cuts in on beat 3 of bar 34 (83.75 s) so the full sign-off ("Petros AI-Tech Agency. Innovate smarter. Create beyond limits.") ends before 90 s; the final chord sits on the cut.
- **Mira** is 6 cm tall (not 4.5) so she reads at phone size; a chair stands behind her at the kitchen table; the armchair moved to the front so close-ups of the work table are shot from between the chair and the table.
- **The listing tag** stands on the table; its text is "Speckled Sky Mug / hand-thrown". The reply bubble reads "Yes, it's dishwasher safe!".
- **Rendering**: the room's image-based light is kept only on the real mug and the desk (on matte paper it cost a second a frame in software rendering); the lens-blur gather uses 40 taps; no bloom pass.
- **Checks**: every narration line transcribes back (asr_check exit 0); every on-screen word passes the reading-time check (readcheck exit 0).
